Today the installer fills a site with believable demo content from our eleven products and removes it again. After Waves 2 and 3 the same engine builds complete, working sites from ready blueprints, including stacks with third-party plugins like Dokan, and from a plain description the owner types.

Your question

Can we offer "a working multivendor community with Dokan"?

Yes. It is a Wave 2 blueprint, and the installer is built for it.

Since the 6 November refactor, adding a third-party plugin means writing one provider class and one content file. The wizard, Finish, removal, REST and WP-CLI work unchanged. Our cast already has a vendor role, so the sellers come from the same 20 people who post, comment and review across the other products.

What the owner would get

  • WooCommerce and Dokan set up the way their own setup wizards finish
  • Vendor stores with logos, banners and products
  • Sample orders and reviews
  • Store list and shop pages in the menu, full width on Reign or BuddyX
  • Sellers' profiles in BuddyNext and a sellers' forum in Jetonomy
  • Points and badges for selling and reviewing, if Gamification is active

What we would build

  • A WooCommerce provider (products, orders, reviews) that other blueprints reuse
  • A Dokan provider (vendors, stores, store settings), using Pro features only when Dokan Pro is active
  • A content pack: stores and products that fit the community niche
  • The blueprint itself, plus lab cases on Reign and BuddyX

Rules stay the same as Wave 1: only Dokan's and WooCommerce's own functions, never raw database writes; created as the sample vendor, never as the site owner; every object removable.

Being straight with buyers

What "working" means

The blueprint does

  • Installs the structure: pages, menus, categories, spaces, settings
  • Finishes every product's setup and turns off its first-run nags
  • Fills the site so every linked page looks lived in
  • Leaves a real site after "Keep my setup"

The owner still does

  • Connects real payments (we never enter keys)
  • Writes their own Terms and policies (we never publish legal text)
  • Sets up real email sending
  • Swaps in their own brand and content, or keeps ours to start
